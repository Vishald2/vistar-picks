# Air Fryer Buying Guide: What Should You Check Before Buying?

An air fryer has become a popular kitchen appliance for preparing food
with little or no added oil. It can be used for foods such as French
fries, samosas, paneer tikka and roasted vegetables. However, air-fried
food is not exactly the same as deep-fried food, and the results depend
on the appliance, the quantity of food and the cooking method.

Air fryers are available in different sizes and designs. Before buying
one, it is worth comparing more than just the price or the number of
cooking presets. Capacity, wattage, temperature control, basket design,
cleaning and safety all affect everyday use.

This guide explains the main points to check before choosing an air
fryer.

## 1. Understand How an Air Fryer Works

An air fryer is essentially a compact convection cooker. A heating
element warms the air, while a fan circulates hot air around the food.
This helps cook and brown the surface.

You generally need much less oil than you would for deep frying. Some
foods may need only a light coating or spray of oil, while other foods
can be cooked without adding oil. The result, however, will not always
have the same texture as food cooked in a deep fryer.

Air fryers are useful for reheating, roasting and cooking smaller
portions. They can also be convenient when you do not want to heat a
full-size oven.

## 2. Choose the Right Capacity

Capacity is one of the most important things to consider. A small basket
may be sufficient for one or two people, but it can become inconvenient
when you need to prepare food for a family.

The following table is a rough guide:

  Household size      Approximate capacity
  ----------------- ----------------------
  1--2 people                  2--3 litres
  2--3 people                  3--4 litres
  3--4 people                  4--5 litres
  4--5 people                  5--6 litres
  Larger families         6 litres or more

These figures are only a starting point. The shape of the basket and its
usable cooking area matter as much as the stated litre capacity.

For example, a wide basket may allow you to spread food in a single
layer more easily than a deeper, narrower basket with the same stated
capacity. This matters because overcrowding can restrict airflow and
lead to uneven cooking. You may need to cook in batches if the basket is
too small.

Think about the quantity of food you normally prepare, not just the
number of people in your household.

## 3. Check the Wattage

Most household air fryers are in the approximate range of 1,200 to 2,000
watts, although models can vary.

Wattage indicates the electrical power the appliance can draw. A
higher-wattage model may heat up or recover its temperature more
quickly, but higher wattage does not automatically mean better cooking.
Air circulation, heating design, basket shape and temperature control
also affect performance.

Wattage also matters for electricity use. For example, a 1,500-watt
appliance running continuously for one hour would use 1.5 kilowatt-hours
(kWh). In normal cooking, the heating element cycles on and off, so
actual consumption will depend on the food, temperature and cooking
time.

Before buying, check the appliance's power rating and make sure your
kitchen socket and electrical wiring are suitable for it. Follow the
manufacturer's electrical instructions.

## 4. Look at Temperature Control

Different foods require different cooking temperatures. An adjustable
temperature range gives you more control than a model with only one or
two fixed settings.

Many air fryers offer a range that starts at around 80°C and goes up to
approximately 200°C, but the range varies by model. Check the actual
specifications rather than assuming that every air fryer offers the same
settings.

A timer is also useful because it helps you set the cooking duration.
Some models automatically switch off when the timer ends.

Temperature accuracy and consistency are important. A long list of
presets is less useful if the appliance cannot maintain suitable heat
during cooking.

## 5. Manual or Digital Controls?

Air fryers generally use either manual controls or a digital control
panel.

**Manual controls** usually have dials for setting the temperature and
timer. They are straightforward and may be easier to operate if you
prefer simple controls.

**Digital controls** use buttons or a touch panel. They may offer a
clearer display, more precise settings and preset programmes.

Digital controls are not automatically better at cooking. Choose the
type that you find easy to understand and operate. Also consider whether
the buttons or display will be convenient to use in your kitchen.

## 6. Check the Cooking Functions

Depending on the model, an air fryer may offer several functions, such
as:

-   **Air fry:** Circulates hot air to cook and brown food.
-   **Roast:** Useful for vegetables and other roasted foods.
-   **Bake:** Can be used for selected cakes, muffins and other baked
    items with suitable cookware.
-   **Grill:** Available on some models for particular types of food.
-   **Reheat:** Helps warm previously cooked food.
-   **Dehydrate:** Dries food at a lower temperature over a longer
    period, if the model supports it.

Some models also include preset programmes for foods such as fries,
chicken or vegetables. These presets can make operation easier, but they
are not essential. You can often select the temperature and time
manually.

Do not choose an appliance only because it advertises many cooking
modes. First consider which functions you are actually likely to use.

## 7. Check the Basket Design and Ease of Cleaning

The basket is one of the parts you will handle most often, so its design
deserves attention.

Check the following points:

-   Is the basket easy to remove and put back?
-   Does it have a comfortable, secure handle?
-   Is there a removable crisper plate or tray?
-   Can the basket and accessories be cleaned easily?
-   Are the removable parts dishwasher-safe, according to the
    manufacturer's instructions?

A non-stick coating can make cleaning easier, but it should be handled
carefully. Avoid metal utensils and abrasive scrubbers that may damage
the surface. Use cleaning methods recommended by the manufacturer.

Allow the appliance to cool before cleaning. Do not immerse the main
electrical unit in water.

## 8. Check Important Safety Features

An air fryer operates at high temperatures and uses electrical power.
Safety should be part of your buying decision.

Useful features may include:

-   **Automatic shut-off:** Switches the appliance off when the timer
    ends or when the basket is removed, depending on the design.
-   **Overheat protection:** Helps protect the appliance if it becomes
    excessively hot.
-   **Cool-touch handle:** Reduces the chance of burning your hand while
    handling the basket.
-   **Stable, non-slip base:** Helps keep the appliance steady on the
    countertop.
-   **Secure basket mechanism:** Helps prevent the basket from coming
    loose during use.

Check the manufacturer's safety information, warranty and available
service support. Follow the instructions supplied with the appliance.

During use, place the air fryer on a stable, heat-resistant surface and
leave the ventilation space recommended by the manufacturer. Keep it
away from curtains, paper and other flammable materials. Do not block
its air outlets.

## 9. Single-Basket or Dual-Basket Air Fryer?

Most basic air fryers have one cooking basket. Some larger models have
two separate baskets.

A **single-basket air fryer** is usually simpler and may be sufficient
for everyday cooking in a small or medium household. If you want to cook
different foods one after another, you can use the same basket in
separate batches.

A **dual-basket air fryer** allows you to cook two foods separately at
the same time. Some models let you set different temperatures and
cooking times for each basket. Certain models also offer a
synchronisation feature so that both baskets finish cooking together.

Dual-basket models can be useful for larger households or when you
regularly prepare different foods at once. They generally take up more
counter space and may cost more.

Choose a dual-basket model only if you expect to use the additional
space and flexibility.

## 10. What Should You Check Before Buying?

Use this checklist when comparing different models:

-   [ ] Capacity is suitable for the quantity of food you normally
    prepare.
-   [ ] Basket shape provides enough usable cooking area.
-   [ ] Wattage is suitable for your kitchen's electrical setup.
-   [ ] Temperature and timer settings meet your cooking needs.
-   [ ] Controls are easy for you to operate.
-   [ ] The cooking functions are useful rather than just numerous.
-   [ ] Basket and removable parts are easy to clean.
-   [ ] Safety features and operating instructions are clear.
-   [ ] Warranty and service support are available.
-   [ ] The appliance fits comfortably on your kitchen counter.

## Conclusion

The right air fryer is not necessarily the biggest, most expensive or
the one with the most presets. It is the one that suits your cooking
habits and household size.

Pay particular attention to usable basket space, ease of cleaning,
temperature control and safety. Compare the specifications of a few
models, read the manufacturer's instructions and choose an appliance
that you can use comfortably in your everyday kitchen.
